**Pierre-Louis Biojout (PLB)** @plbiojout [2026-01-29](https://x.com/plbiojout/status/2016920022279483821)

For the first time, I built a product for myself. It changed everything.

In the past, I built for others:

\- Built products for enterprise customers, used by CAC 40 companies

\- Worked with research labs to make their AI models better

\- Shipped robotics developer kits used by NVIDIA and Hitachi

\- Even deployed AI models on excavators in Texas

It’s hard to build truly exceptional products if you don’t use them every day.

A few months ago, I was looking to build my new personal operating stack. The software and AI agents to automate and scale most of my personal and professional life.

I tried many products. All of them were missing something:

⚫ I don't want to be in the loop. I don't want a copilot. I want agents working for me while I'm sleeping or at the gym.

⚫ I want them to be stateful. Have memory, take feedback, become exponentially better. I don't want to just "do the work". I want to build systems that compound.

⚫ I don't want to change my workflows. They use my tools. Slack, Notion, Github, Qonto, Brex, Gmail, iMessage. On your first day at a company, you don't impose new tools on everyone.

⚫ I want to choose the level of trust. On your first day, you won't get access to my crypto wallet or the password to my Apple account. Low-trust first, autonomy over time.

So I decided to build it for myself. A tool where I can create agents and have them work for me.

Started with my personal life. Personal doctor, Polymarket arbitrage opportunities checker, an agent reading newsletters watching all the videos and listening to all the podcasts I'll never get to, one checking my email for critical admin stuff that should have been done a long time ago, a personal assistant helping me find a nice place to rent in San Francisco.

I started to use it every day. Felt frustrated when the product was down.

Then I started using it at my company. An agent pleasing the accountant by pulling receipts from my Gmail, one monitoring the web and Twitter for competitors and new ideas, another managing our Notion database of users and waitlist, one watching Sentry alerts and suggesting code fixes directly in GitHub, one reaching out to people on Linkedin.

That's when something clicked. The product started fueling itself. When the product gets better, the company building the product gets better. Which makes the product better. Repeat.

Showed it to some friends. Some wanted in, I said OK for a 10-person private alpha. The 10 became 20, which became 30. Someone even onboarded his whole company (chill out, it's 4 people).

1,800 agent runs later, the gatekeeping is over. I'm now releasing to anyone who wants to delegate work and build a system that grows.

No launch video. No landing page. No billboard on Times Square. No slide deck.

Just the product.
